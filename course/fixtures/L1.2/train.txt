Once upon a time, in a town by the sea, there lived a small robot named Pip.
Pip liked to count things. He counted the boats in the harbor, the gulls on the roof, and the stones on the path.
Every morning Pip said, "Today I will count something new." And every morning he did.
One day Pip found a box on the beach. The box was blue, and it was locked.
"What is inside?" asked Mia, the baker's daughter. "I don't know," said Pip, "but I'll find out."
They carried the box to the bakery. Mia's father looked at it and laughed. "That's an old toolbox," he said.
"It belonged to the lighthouse keeper. He's been gone for twelve years, but his things still wash up now and then."
Pip turned the box over and over. On the bottom were numbers: 3, 1, 4, 1, 5.
"It's a code," said Mia. "We've seen numbers like that before. They're the start of pi!"
Pip pressed the buttons: 3-1-4-1-5. The lock clicked, and the lid opened slowly.
Inside there was a map, a brass key, and a note. The note said: "Follow the light. Count the steps. Don't give up."
So they went to the lighthouse. The door was heavy, and the stairs were steep.
Pip counted the steps as they climbed: one, two, three, four... ninety-nine, one hundred, one hundred and one.
At the top they found a small door. The brass key fit the lock perfectly.
Behind the door was a room full of old lamps, books, and a telescope that pointed at the sea.
"Look," said Mia, "the books are full of numbers too." Pip opened one. It was a logbook of ships.
Every ship that passed the lighthouse was written down: its name, the date, and the time.
The last entry said: "The Morning Star, 14 May, 06:30. All is well."
Pip and Mia read the logbook until the sun went down. They counted 1,024 ships in all.
"Why did he count them?" asked Mia. "Because counting is how you remember," said Pip.
From that day on, Pip and Mia kept the logbook. Every morning they climbed the stairs, and every morning they counted the ships.
The town was proud of them. The baker made a cake shaped like a lighthouse, and everyone came to eat it.
Pip didn't eat cake, of course, but he counted the slices: there were twenty-four.
In the winter the sea was grey and the ships were few. Pip counted them anyway.
In the spring the gulls came back, and the harbor was busy again.
Mia grew older, and Pip grew rusty, but they never stopped counting.
And when Mia's own daughter was born, the first thing Pip said was, "Today I will count something new."
The end.

Once there was a keeper who lived at the edge of the sea. Every evening she
climbed the stairs of the tower, wound the clock that turned the lamp, and
watched the beam sweep across the water. The ships that passed in the night
never saw her, but they saw the light, and the light was enough.

One winter the clock began to slow. The beam turned late, then later, and the
keeper could not tell why. She took the clock apart on the kitchen table and
laid every gear in a line. She counted the teeth on each wheel and wrote the
numbers in a notebook. The numbers were right. The gears were clean. Still the
lamp turned late.

On the third night she sat beside the clock and listened. Between the ticks
there was a second sound, small and dry, like sand poured onto paper. She
followed it down the stairs to the base of the tower, where the weight that
drove the clock hung on its chain. Salt had crusted on the chain, link by link,
until the weight could not fall freely. She cleaned each link with warm water
and a stiff brush, and when she was done the weight fell as smoothly as it had
on the day the tower was built.

The keeper wrote down what she had found, so that the next keeper would not
need three nights to find it. She wrote what the lamp did when the chain was
dirty, how she had found the cause, and what she had done to fix it. Then she
added one more line: clean the chain at the start of every winter, before the
clock begins to slow.

Years later a young keeper came to the tower. The first winter the lamp turned
late, and the young keeper opened the notebook and read the old notes. The
chain was clean by nightfall. Out at sea the ships passed in the dark, and they
saw the light turning on time, and they never knew how close it had come to
stopping.

Once the keeper had gone, the notebook stayed on the shelf by the stairs. Each
keeper who came after added a page: a storm that broke a window, a gull that
nested in the lamp room, a summer when the oil ran low. None of the pages were
long. Each one said what happened, why it happened, and what to do the next
time. The tower stood for a hundred years, and the light never went out.
